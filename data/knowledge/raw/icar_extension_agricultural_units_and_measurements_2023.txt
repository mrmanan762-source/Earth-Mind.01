1. LAND AND AREA UNITS
1 hectare (ha) = 10,000 square metres = 2.471 acres. 1 acre = 0.4047 ha. 1 bigha varies
by state (commonly 0.25-0.4 ha). Land is often described in hectares for recommendations.

2. YIELD AND NUTRIENT UNITS
Crop yield is expressed in tonnes per hectare (t/ha) or quintals per hectare (q/ha);
1 quintal = 100 kg. Fertilizer nutrients are expressed as N, P2O5 and K2O. Fertilizer
doses are given in kg per hectare.

3. FERTILIZER SOURCES (indicative nutrient content)
Urea (46 percent N), diammonium phosphate (18 percent N, 46 percent P2O5), single
superphosphate (16 percent P2O5), muriate of potash (60 percent K2O), sulphate of potash
(50 percent K2O), and gypsum (calcium and sulphur).

4. IRRIGATION AND WATER MANAGEMENT CONCEPTS
Irrigation supplies water to meet crop evapotranspiration. Key concepts: crop water
requirement, irrigation scheduling (interval or critical-stage based), and irrigation
efficiency. Drip and sprinkler systems improve water-use efficiency over flood irrigation.

5. WEATHER AND CLIMATE CONCEPTS
Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions (temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind);
climate is the long-term pattern. Crops have cardinal temperatures and critical moisture
stages. Monsoon onset and distribution strongly influence kharif cropping.

6. FARMING SYSTEMS AND SUSTAINABILITY
Farming systems combine crops, livestock and other enterprises. Sustainable agriculture
integrates soil health, water conservation, biodiversity and economic viability.

7. INTEGRATED NUTRIENT AND PEST MANAGEMENT
Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) combines organic and inorganic sources to match
crop demand. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines cultural, biological and
chemical methods with economic thresholds to reduce pesticide use.

8. POST-HARVEST HANDLING AND STORAGE
Timely harvesting, cleaning, drying to safe moisture content, grading and proper storage
reduce post-harvest losses. Store grains in clean, dry, aerated structures; control
storage pests with hygiene and approved fumigants where permitted.

9. AGRICULTURAL ECONOMICS CONCEPTS
Key concepts include cost of cultivation, gross and net returns, benefit-cost ratio,
market price and minimum support price. Economics vary with yield, input costs and market
conditions.

10. VERIFIED STATISTICS GUIDANCE
Agricultural statistics change over time. For current area, production and yield figures,
retrieve data from live authoritative sources (for example, the Directorate of Economics
and Statistics, Government of India) rather than relying on static documents.
