1. CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum L.) is a long-duration tropical/subtropical crop;
optimum temperature 26-32 degrees C. It requires 1000-1500 mm water. Deep, well-drained
loam to clay loam with pH 6.5-7.5 and good organic matter is ideal.

2. GROWTH STAGES
Germination, tillering, grand growth, maturity and ripening. Tillering and grand growth
are the most nutrient- and water-demanding stages.

3. PLANTING MATERIAL, SPACING AND PLANTING
Use healthy, disease-free two-to-three budded setts from 8-10 month old cane. Seed rate
about 30,000-40,000 two-budded setts/ha. Spacing 75-90 cm in furrows (or paired-row
planting). Autumn planting (September-October) and spring planting (February-March).
Treat setts with fungicide against red rot and smut.

4. NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (general principles)
Apply FYM 10-20 t/ha. General recommendation 150-250:60-100:60-120 kg N:P2O5:K2O
per ha depending on soil and duration; apply N in splits (basal, tillering, grand growth).
Include micronutrients (zinc, iron, manganese) where deficient.

5. WATER REQUIREMENT
Requires 1200-1800 mm water. Irrigate at 7-12 day intervals; critical stages are
germination, tillering and grand growth. Avoid moisture stress during grand growth.

6. COMMON PESTS
Early shoot borer (Chilo infuscatellus), top borer (Scirpophaga excerptalis), internode
borer, stalk borer, pyrilla, woolly aphid, white grub, termites.

7. COMMON DISEASES AND SYMPTOMS
Red rot (Colletotrichum falcatum): reddish discolouration with cross bands and sour smell.
Smut (Sporisorium scitamineum): black whip-like structure. Wilt: drying of canes. Grassy
shoot disease (phytoplasma). Ratoon stunting.

8. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
Use disease-free setts and resistant varieties; hot-water/fungicide sett treatment; trash
mulching and earthing up; release Trichogramma and natural enemies for borers; rotate
and remove infected clumps; avoid excessive nitrogen.

9. HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
Harvest at maturity (10-12 months, or 12-18 months for some tropical varieties) when
brix and sucrose peak. Harvest close to ground level; crush within 24-48 hours to avoid
sucrose loss; retain healthy stubble for ratoon.

10. CROP CALENDAR (indicative)
Autumn planting: September-October; Spring planting: February-March. Harvest begins
October-November through the crushing season (October-April).
