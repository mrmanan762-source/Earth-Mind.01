1. CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Indian mustard (Brassica juncea L.) is a cool-season rabi oilseed; optimum temperature
10-25 degrees C. It tolerates light frost but is sensitive to high temperature at pod
filling. Well-drained sandy loam to clay loam with pH 6.0-7.5 is ideal.

2. GROWTH STAGES
Germination, vegetative growth, branching, flowering, siliqua (pod) formation and seed
filling. Flowering and pod filling are critical for oil yield.

3. SEED RATE, SPACING AND SOWING
Seed rate 4-6 kg/ha; spacing 30 cm x 10-15 cm. Sow in October (timely sown) to early
November. Late sowing reduces yield. Treat seed against Alternaria blight and white rust.

4. NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (general principles)
General recommendation 60-80:30-40:20-30 kg N:P2O5:K2O per ha; apply half N and full
P, K as basal, remaining N in one or two splits. Apply 20-30 kg sulphur per ha, which is
important for oil content and yield.

5. WATER REQUIREMENT
Requires 250-400 mm water. Provide light irrigation at branching, flowering and siliqua
formation. Two irrigations (pre-flowering and pod filling) are generally adequate.

6. COMMON PESTS
Mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi), the most damaging pest; painted bug (Bagrada
cruciferarum); sawfly; flea beetle; leaf miner.

7. COMMON DISEASES AND SYMPTOMS
Alternaria blight (Alternaria brassicae): dark concentric spots on leaves and pods.
White rust (Albugo candida): white pustules and staghead. Downy mildew: pale patches
with fungal growth. Sclerotinia rot in humid conditions.

8. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
Sow early to escape aphid peak; grow tolerant varieties; manage aphids with recommended
insecticides at economic threshold; use yellow sticky traps; remove infected plants; rotate
with non-cruciferous crops.

9. HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
Harvest when 75 percent siliquae turn yellow-brown to avoid shattering. Sun-dry, thresh
and store at 8-10 percent moisture.

10. CROP CALENDAR (indicative)
Rabi: sow October-early November, harvest February-March.
