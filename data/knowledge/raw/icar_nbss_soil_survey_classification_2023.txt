1. SOIL CLASSIFICATION (major orders, indicative)
Soils are classified into orders including Entisols (recent, weakly developed),
Inceptisols (young soils with weak horizon development), Alfisols (moderately leached
with clay accumulation), Vertisols (swelling black soils), Aridisols (dry soils),
Ultisols and Mollisols. Indian soils are commonly described as alluvial, black (regur),
red, laterite, desert, mountain and saline-alkaline soils.

2. SOIL TEXTURE
Texture is the relative proportion of sand, silt and clay. The textural triangle defines
classes: sand, loamy sand, sandy loam, loam, silt loam, clay loam and clay. Texture
governs water holding capacity, drainage, aeration and nutrient retention. Sandy soils
drain fast and hold few nutrients; clay soils hold water and nutrients but drain slowly.

3. SOIL STRUCTURE
Structure is the arrangement of soil particles into aggregates (granular, blocky,
prismatic, platy, single grain, massive). Good granular structure improves porosity,
infiltration and root growth. Organic matter and calcium promote stable aggregates.

4. SOIL pH
Soil pH measures acidity/alkalinity on a 0-14 scale. Most crops grow best at pH 6.0-7.5.
Acidic soils (pH < 6.0) may need liming; alkaline soils (pH > 8.5) may need gypsum and
organic matter. pH affects nutrient availability: nitrogen, phosphorus and micronutrients
are most available in near-neutral soils.

5. SALINITY AND SODICITY
Saline soils have high soluble salts (measured by electrical conductivity, EC) and
appear white with crusts; they reduce water uptake. Sodic soils have high exchangeable
sodium (measured by exchangeable sodium percentage, ESP) and poor structure. Reclamation
uses gypsum, drainage and salt-tolerant crops.

6. ORGANIC CARBON AND SOIL FERTILITY
Soil organic carbon (SOC) is central to soil fertility, structure and microbial activity.
Low SOC (< 0.5 percent) indicates poor fertility. Practices that build SOC include adding
FYM/compost, green manuring, crop residue retention and reduced tillage.

7. MACRONUTRIENTS AND MICRONUTRIENTS
Primary macronutrients: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K). Secondary:
calcium, magnesium, sulphur. Micronutrients: zinc, iron, manganese, copper, boron,
molybdenum, chlorine. Deficiencies cause characteristic symptoms (for example, zinc
deficiency causes khaira in rice; iron chlorosis in high-pH soils).

8. SOIL TESTING AND REPORT INTERPRETATION
Soil testing measures pH, EC, organic carbon, available N, P, K and micronutrients.
Ratings are commonly low, medium or high. Interpretation converts test values into
fertilizer recommendations using soil test crop response (STCR) equations where available.
Always pair a soil-test report with the crop, target yield and local recommendations.

9. SOIL-CROP RELATIONSHIPS
Crop choice should match soil texture, depth, drainage and pH. Rice suits clayey soils
with water retention; groundnut and potato prefer well-drained sandy loams; cotton
performs well on black soils; tea and some horticultural crops prefer acidic soils.

10. SOIL MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
Maintain organic matter; balance nutrients using soil tests; avoid excessive tillage;
prevent erosion with contour bunding, cover crops and mulching; manage salinity with
drainage and gypsum; and rotate crops to break pest cycles and improve soil health.
