1. CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Field pea (Pisum sativum L.) is a cool-season rabi pulse; optimum temperature 10-25
degrees C. Frost at flowering damages the crop. Well-drained loam to clay loam with
pH 6.0-7.5 is ideal; it is sensitive to waterlogging and salinity.

2. GROWTH STAGES
Germination, vegetative growth, branching, flowering, pod formation and pod filling.
Flowering and pod filling are the most moisture-sensitive stages.

3. SEED RATE, SPACING AND SOWING
Seed rate 75-100 kg/ha depending on seed size; spacing 30 cm x 10 cm. Sow in October-
November. Inoculate seed with Rhizobium and treat with Trichoderma against soil-borne
diseases.

4. NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (general principles)
Being a legume, pea fixes atmospheric nitrogen; apply a starter dose of 20-25 kg N/ha.
General recommendation 20:50:20 kg N:P2O5:K2O per ha. Apply 10-15 kg sulphur per ha
where deficient. Rhizobium inoculation improves nitrogen fixation.

5. WATER REQUIREMENT
Requires 300-400 mm water. Provide light irrigation at branching, flowering and pod
filling. Avoid excess irrigation which promotes vegetative growth and lodging.

6. COMMON PESTS
Pea aphid (Acyrthosiphon pisum), pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera), leaf miner, pea
weevil, thrips.

7. COMMON DISEASES AND SYMPTOMS
Powdery mildew (Erysiphe pisi): white powdery growth on leaves and stems. Wilt (Fusarium
oxysporum f. sp. pisi): yellowing and wilting. Rust (Uromyces pisi): reddish-brown
pustules. Downy mildew: pale patches with fungal growth.

8. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
Use wilt-resistant varieties; rotate with cereals; treat seed with fungicide against wilt
and root rot; manage aphids and pod borer at threshold; encourage natural enemies; destroy
crop residues.

9. HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
Harvest pods at green immature stage for vegetables, or at maturity (pods turn yellow-brown)
for dry seed. Thresh and dry seed to 12 percent moisture for storage.

10. CROP CALENDAR (indicative)
Rabi: sow October-November, harvest February-March (green) or March-April (dry seed).
