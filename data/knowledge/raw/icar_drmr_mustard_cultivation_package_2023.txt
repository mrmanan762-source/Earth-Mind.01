1. SPECIES IDENTITY, ECOTYPES & GEOGRAPHIC DISTRIBUTION
Rapeseed-mustard represents the second most vital edible oilseed complex in India. Cultivated species exhibit distinct genomic profiles based on U's Triangle:
- Indian Mustard (Brassica juncea [L.] Czern. & Coss., 2n=36): Predominant species (>80% area). High yield potential, heat/drought tolerance, adaptive elasticity across Rajasthan, Haryana, MP, UP, Gujarat, Punjab.
- Yellow Sarson (Brassica rapa L. var. yellow sarson): Self-compatible erect/spreading types, bold yellow seeds, high oil content (42–45%), high thermal sensitivity. Prevalent in Assam, Bihar, WB, Odisha.
- Brown Sarson (Brassica rapa L. var. brown sarson): Includes Lotni (self-incompatible, high frost tolerance in northern hills) and Toria (ultra-short duration 70–90 day catch crop).
- Gobhi Sarson (Brassica napus L.): Long-duration (150–160 days), high yield potential, grown under irrigated conditions in Punjab, Haryana, HP.
- Karan Rai (Brassica carinata A. Braun): Ethiopian mustard, high resistance to Alternaria blight and white rust, late maturing.
- Taramira (Eruca sativa Mill.): Exceptional drought and soil salinity tolerance, low-input dryland crop in Rajasthan and Haryana.

2. CLIMATE, SOIL & SOWING WINDOWS
- Climate & Temperature Sensitivity: Cool, dry Rabi season crop. Optimum germination temperature: 25°C–28°C; vegetative/flowering stage: 15°C–25°C. Sensitive to severe frost (<3°C) and cloudy/humid weather at flowering.
- Optimal Sowing Windows: Sowing between October 1 and October 25 is critical for Indian Mustard to escape peak aphid infestation and Alternaria blight. Sowing window for Toria: Sept 15 to Oct 5.
- Soil Requirements: Thrives in light to heavy loams, sandy loams, and alluvial soils with pH 6.0–8.0. Highly sensitive to waterlogging at seedling and early branching stages.

3. VARIETIES, DENSITY & SEED TREATMENT
- Recommended Varieties (Brassica juncea):
  * Giriraj / DRMRIJ 31 (ICAR-DRMR): Zone II (Rajasthan, Haryana, Delhi, SW Punjab), maturity 135–140 days, yield 22–26 q/ha, high oil (40–41%).
  * RH 725 (CCS HAU Hisar): Bold seeded, yield potential 25–28 q/ha, maturity 138–143 days.
  * NRCHB-101 (ICAR-DRMR): Rajasthan, UP, MP, yield 20–24 q/ha, early vigor.
  * Pusa Jaikisan / Bio-902 (ICAR-IARI): Somaclonal variant, bold seeded, high yield (22–25 q/ha).
  * PM-32 / Pusa Mustard 32 (ICAR-IARI): Canola quality double-low (<2% erucic acid in oil, <30 μmol/g glucosinolates in meal).
  * RGN-73 / RGN-48 (ARS Sri Ganganagar): Rajasthan, Haryana irrigated plains, yield 22–25 q/ha.
- Seed Rate & Spacing: 3.5–5.0 kg/ha seeded at 30–45 cm row spacing and 10–15 cm plant-to-plant spacing (thinning at 15–20 DAS). Sowing depth: 2–3 cm into moist soil.
- Seed Treatment: Treat seed with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg seed or Metalaxyl 35 SD @ 6 g/kg seed (protects against white rust & downy mildew) + Imidacloprid 70 WS @ 5 g/kg seed (protects against early flea beetle and aphids).

4. NUTRIENT & SULPHUR MANAGEMENT
- Fertilizer Recommendations:
  * Irrigated Regime: Basal N:P2O5:K2O @ 80–90:40:40 kg/ha. Apply 50% N + 100% PK at sowing, top-dress remaining 50% N at 1st irrigation (25–30 DAS).
  * Rainfed Regime: Basal N:P2O5:K2O @ 40–50:20:20 kg/ha at sowing.
- Sulphur Application (Critical for Oil Synthesis): Apply Elemental Sulphur @ 20–40 kg/ha or Gypsum @ 150–250 kg/ha at land preparation. Sulphur increases oil content by 2–4% and seed yield by 15–25%. Apply Zinc Sulfate @ 25 kg/ha in Zn-deficient soils.

5. IRRIGATION & WATER MANAGEMENT
- Critical Stages: Requires 2 light irrigations (3–5 cm depth). 1st irrigation at flowering/branching initiation (25–30 DAS); 2nd irrigation at pod formation / siliquae development stage (50–60 DAS).
- Phytosanitary Irrigation Pause: Avoid irrigations between December 25 and January 15 across Northern India to minimize Sclerotinia stem rot infection, micro-climatic humidity, and frost susceptibility.

6. INTEGRATED PEST & DISEASE MANAGEMENT (IPDM)
- Weed Control: Pre-emergence Pendimethalin 30% EC @ 1.0 kg a.i./ha (3.3 L/ha) in 500 L water within 3 days of sowing + 1 hand weeding at 25–30 DAS.
- Major Insect Pests:
  * Mustard Aphid (Lipaphis erysimi): Nymphs/adults suck sap from central shoots/siliquae. ETL: 20–25 aphids/10 cm central shoot or 1.5–2.0 cm infested shoot length on 20% plants. Spray Dimethoate 30% EC @ 1.0 L/ha or Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.25 ml/L or Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.2 g/L. Spray during late afternoon to protect honeybee pollinators.
  * Painted Bug (Bagrada hilaris) & Flea Beetle: Attack early seedlings. Drench/spray Chlorpyrifos 20% EC @ 1.5 L/ha.
- Major Diseases:
  * Sclerotinia Stem Rot (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum): Water-soaked lesions on stems, white cottony mycelium, black sclerotia. Spray Carbendazim 50% WP @ 1.0 g/L or Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L at 45–50 DAS (first bloom stage).
  * Alternaria Blight (Alternaria brassicae): Concentric target spots on leaves and siliquae. Spray Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L or Iprodione 50% WP @ 2.0 g/L at 40 and 60 DAS.
  * White Rust (Albugo candida): White pustules on leaves, staghead floral malformation. Seed treatment with Metalaxyl 35 SD @ 6 g/kg seed; spray Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP @ 2.5 g/L at 50 DAS.
- Pollinator Integration: Honeybees (Apis cerana, Apis mellifera) enhance cross-pollination, increasing seed set and oil yield by 15–25%. Avoid toxic chemical sprays during peak morning foraging (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM).

7. HARVESTING, THRESHING & ECONOMICS
- Harvest Maturity: Harvest when 75–80% of siliquae turn golden-yellow/straw-colored (seed moisture 15–18%). Cut plants, bundle, and sun-dry on clean threshing floors for 4–6 days.
- Seed Drying & Storage: Thresh using pulse/mustard thresher. Dry seeds below 8.0% moisture to prevent oil rancidity and fungal decay during storage.
- Macro-Economics: Cluster Frontline Demonstrations (CFLDs) record a mean yield of 1.85–2.20 t/ha vs traditional farmer practices (1.45–1.65 t/ha). Cost of cultivation: ₹24,000–28,000/ha; Gross returns: ₹75,000–95,000/ha; Net profit: ₹55,000–72,000/ha; Benefit-Cost (B:C) ratio: 2.8–3.4.
