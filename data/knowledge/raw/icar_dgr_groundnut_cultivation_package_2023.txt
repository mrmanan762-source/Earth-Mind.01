1. CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea L.) is a warm-season oilseed; optimum temperature 25-30
degrees C. It requires 500-700 mm rainfall. Well-drained sandy loam and loamy sand with
pH 6.0-7.5 and adequate calcium are ideal; heavy soils hinder pegging and pod
development.

2. GROWTH STAGES
Germination, vegetative growth, flowering, pegging, pod development and maturity.
Pegging and pod development are the most critical and moisture-sensitive stages.

3. SEED RATE, SPACING AND SOWING
Seed rate 100-120 kg/ha (kernel basis); spacing 30 cm x 10 cm. Sow with onset of
monsoon in June-July (kharif) or January-February (summer). Treat seed with
Trichoderma and Rhizobium; treat with fungicide against collar rot.

4. NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (general principles)
Apply FYM 5-10 t/ha. General recommendation 25:50:50 kg N:P2O5:K2O per ha. Apply
gypsum 250-400 kg/ha at flowering to supply calcium and sulphur. Apply 20-25 kg
sulphur per ha where deficient; groundnut benefits from calcium for pod filling.

5. WATER REQUIREMENT
Requires 500-700 mm water. Critical stages: flowering and pegging (avoid moisture stress)
and pod development. Provide protective irrigation at 10-12 day intervals where irrigated.

6. COMMON PESTS
Leaf miner (Aproaerema modicella), white grub, termites, thrips, aphids, jassids.

7. COMMON DISEASES AND SYMPTOMS
Tikka (early and late leaf spot, Cercospora spp.): dark brown to black leaf spots.
Rust (Puccinia arachidis): reddish-brown pustules. Collar rot (Aspergillus niger):
seedling collar rotting. Stem rot (Sclerotium rolfsii): white mycelium at base. Bud
necrosis virus (aphid-borne).

8. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
Use disease-resistant varieties and treated seed; rotate with cereals; deep summer
ploughing; manage leaf miner and thrips at threshold; use pheromone traps for white grub;
remove and destroy infected crop debris; manage aphids to reduce virus spread.

9. HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
Harvest when inner shell shows dark veins and kernels are mature. Dry pods to 8-9
percent moisture before storage to prevent aflatoxin development.

10. CROP CALENDAR (indicative)
Kharif: sow June-July, harvest October-November. Summer: sow January-February,
harvest May-June.
