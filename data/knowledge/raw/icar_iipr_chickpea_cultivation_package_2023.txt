1. CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.) is a cool-season rabi pulse; optimum temperature 20-25
degrees C. It tolerates drought but is sensitive to waterlogging and high temperature at
podding. Well-drained sandy loam to clay loam with pH 6.0-8.0 is suitable.

2. GROWTH STAGES
Germination, vegetative growth, branching, flowering, pod formation and pod filling.
Flowering and pod filling require adequate soil moisture; heat stress at podding reduces
yield.

3. SEED RATE, SPACING AND SOWING
Seed rate 60-80 kg/ha (desi) and 80-100 kg/ha (kabuli); spacing 30 cm x 10 cm. Sow with
the retreat of monsoon in October-November. Treat seed with Rhizobium and Trichoderma.

4. NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (general principles)
Apply a starter dose of 20-25 kg N/ha; general recommendation 20:50:30 kg N:P2O5:K2O
per ha. Apply 10-20 kg sulphur per ha where deficient. Rhizobium inoculation improves
nitrogen fixation.

5. WATER REQUIREMENT
Requires 250-350 mm water. Chickpea is largely rainfed; provide protective irrigation at
branching and pod filling where irrigation is available. Avoid excess irrigation.

6. COMMON PESTS
Pod borer (Helicoverpa armigera), the most destructive pest; termites; cutworms; aphids.

7. COMMON DISEASES AND SYMPTOMS
Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceris): yellowing and wilting. Ascochyta blight:
grey-brown lesions with pycnidia. Dry root rot (Rhizoctonia bataticola): sudden drying.
Botrytis grey mould in cool humid weather.

8. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
Use wilt-resistant varieties; rotate with cereals; deep summer ploughing against termites;
install pheromone traps and bird perches for pod borer; use HaNPV and recommended
insecticides at economic threshold; remove and destroy infected plants.

9. HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
Harvest when plants turn yellow-brown and pods mature. Thresh and dry seed to 9-12
percent moisture. Store in clean, dry structures; follow fumigation where needed.

10. CROP CALENDAR (indicative)
Rabi: sow October-November, harvest February-March.
